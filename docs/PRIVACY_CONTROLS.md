# Local privacy and deliberate deletion (#31)

Settings explains that attendance, offices and settings stay on this device, with no account, analytics, export or sync in this preview. Attendance and history work offline. The only network exception is user-driven office setup: a submitted address may reach the device geocoder, and the reviewed map area loads OpenStreetMap tiles; attendance history is not sent. Android Auto Backup and device transfer are disabled in the packaged configuration. Uninstalling Hamster Cage or clearing its app storage deletes local data, and the app cannot recover it. An ordinary same-package update or restart does not request deletion. The empty first-run Dashboard links to this local-data disclosure before office setup.

Two actions have separate scope previews and deliberate confirmations, each stating that the app cannot recover the deleted data. **Delete attendance and calendar history** removes immutable raw transitions, corrections, manual sessions, excluded dates/reasons/notes, WFH labels, capture coverage/review dates and delivery health. It retains configured offices and base policy (timezone, target, weekdays, short-gap and open-session rules). Current totals and open sessions are derived afresh from the remaining empty history. **Reset all app data** asks Android to clear the app's private data, including offices and base policy, and revoke acquired permissions; the app may close and must be reopened. Android's [`ActivityManager.clearApplicationUserData()`](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/app/ActivityManager#clearApplicationUserData()) reports whether the clear request was accepted, not a synchronous proof that every byte is already gone. The UI hides old data after acceptance until restart.

History deletion is a journal-first operation, not an ordinary correction. A versioned app-private DataStore record moves to `pending` and increments a generation before the Room transaction. Room deletes all five attendance/calendar tables in one transaction; office rows and policy preferences are untouched. Coverage and delivery health are cleared next; only then does the journal become idle. A failed Room transaction rolls back all facts. If any later step fails or the process ends, the pending marker remains and the next process retries deletion before presenting source-derived attendance. Only a completely empty journal represents a fresh install; a partial, unreadable or unsupported record hides attendance and blocks capture without rewriting its bytes. Settings offers a retry or a separately confirmed full reset.

One process mutex serializes the complete geofence callback check→fact insert→coverage update, registration reconciliation and deletion sequence. The callback action includes the persisted generation after a history delete; queued prior-generation callbacks, including those without a trustworthy fix time, are ignored. A durable cursor advances after each successful removal of a prior-generation PendingIntent. Re-registration retries only generations after that cursor, including a failed older removal across another deletion/process restart, and never retires the current live generation. The healthy path stays bounded after repeated deletes. Current-day presence remains unknown until a real post-reset observation arrives. In-flight correction and calendar actions are checked against the generation captured by their editor and serialized with the reset; the editor state is recreated when generation changes.

The receiver's bounded wait on that mutex can itself expire while Play Services reconciliation runs. A timed-out callback is recorded as a durable delivery failure and coverage outage outside the blocked gate; it cannot be treated as a healthy transition. Dashboard presence additionally requires no current delivery failure, even if registration later succeeds while an earlier same-day observation remains in the ledger. A real later observation is needed to restore confidence.

The Activity presents privacy, repository and coverage state as one generation-keyed flow. Pending/Unavailable emits hidden storage; when the journal becomes idle, the flow first emits Loading and opens fresh cold Room and coverage queries. Older attendance or coverage values cannot be paired with a newer idle journal generation. A delayed-query UI regression keeps the old open session hidden while Room is delayed, then keeps the screen Loading while coverage is delayed; the fresh empty record finally shows unknown history rather than a falsely complete zero.

Focused tests cover cancel/confirm and both previews, a failed Room transaction with all facts retained, deletion of every promised fact and an open session with office/policy retention after fresh database/DataStore reopen, journal failure/retry and malformed partial records after a fresh DataStore scope, and distinct old/new PendingIntent identities. The journal recovery and Room rollback/reopen tests use separate fixtures; they do not emulate a process crash between those exact production steps. On an API 37 emulator, a synthetic office fixture was present before full reset; confirming the scope preview terminated the app process and removed its private database directory. Reopening created a fresh database and showed the empty-office setup state. No personal data fixture was used. Actual secure physical-media erasure is an OS responsibility and is not claimed.
