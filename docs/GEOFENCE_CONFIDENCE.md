# Office-area boundary confidence (#101)

Android recommends a geofence radius of at least 100–150 meters and notes that larger areas work better for home/work style places. Background geofence delivery can take minutes. New Hamster Cage offices default to **200 m** as a stability-oriented office-area boundary. The allowed 50–5000 m range remains under user control: saved radii are never rewritten. Setup and saved-office cards warn below 150 m and when two enabled attendance office areas touch or overlap. The app never interprets a geofence as a building wall or badge event. [Android guidance](https://developer.android.com/develop/sensors-and-location/location/geofencing)

Every Play Services ENTER/EXIT remains an immutable local raw fact. When the triggering location includes usable accuracy, the app stores the accuracy in meters with receipt and observed-fix times, but stores no coordinates. Bounded foreground, adaptive and periodic fixes also retain their accuracy with appended presence/absence facts. An inconclusive bounded fix stores only its last accuracy and sanitized outcome in app-private monitoring diagnostics; it does not manufacture a PRESENCE/ABSENCE fact. A fix can classify one office as inside only if its whole accuracy circle lies within that eligible office and does not plausibly touch another eligible office. A fix wholly outside all eligible offices can establish current absence. Boundary overlap, poor or stale accuracy, approximate-only permission and location-off states do not produce a presence/absence fact. Normal history does not display coordinates, accuracy numbers or raw IDs.

After a *new* geofence boundary delivery commits, the app requests inexact one-shot WorkManager checks under one global unique name, even if the delivery names several offices. A newer delivery replaces pending checks; duplicate delivery does not postpone them. The receiver waits for the enqueue operation while holding its capture gate so older work cannot replace newer work out of order. Each check requests at most one fresh current-location fix with a 15-second request bound and rechecks monitoring, permissions, privacy-reset generation, affected office versions, registration and newest evidence before and after the request. Editing or disabling an affected office or resetting history makes pending work ineffective; opting out cancels it. WorkManager and Android background restrictions may delay, bunch or suppress checks. This is an opportunistic short path, not a guaranteed timer, continuous GPS, exact alarm or foreground service.

For an ENTER, one check runs after about 45 seconds. A fresh, clearly inside fix within three minutes of an isolated same-office EXIT can corroborate continuity; an intervening absence or other-office fact forbids the bridge, and a pre-recovery visit spanning a registration outage is split at the fix instead of credited backward.

### Bounded EXIT verification

An EXIT is a candidate departure, not an immediate boundary. Up to five checks target about 30, 90, 150, 210 and 270 seconds after the EXIT was received, and a fix is accepted only if it was observed within five minutes of that receipt. Inside and outside samples are stored as their own immutable sources and pass the same fix gate as every other check: fresh, precise, and with the whole accuracy circle inside exactly one eligible office or wholly outside all of them. Poor indoor accuracy therefore produces no sample rather than a guess; the gate is not relaxed to make verification succeed.

- **Pending.** While a check can still be accepted, the visit stays open but credit stops at the EXIT's observed time and the Dashboard shows that the exit is awaiting confirmation.
- **Confirmed departure.** Two decisive outside samples at least 45 seconds apart, or a decisive fix at another office, confirm the departure at the raw EXIT time. Verification latency is never credited.
- **Rejected phantom EXIT.** Inside evidence must be continuous after the latest EXIT: at least four decisive inside samples, no stretch from the EXIT's own observation to the first sample or between samples longer than three minutes (the engine's existing confirmation window), the last sample at least four minutes after the EXIT, and no outside sample. Four of the five checks suffice, so one ambiguous indoor fix does not by itself prevent a rejection. Because observation times are compared, both platform delivery latency and late or bunched WorkManager execution count against it. Android documents alert latency as usually under two minutes, about two to three minutes under background location limits and up to six minutes for a stationary device, so a late or bunched set of samples cannot reject an EXIT and never extends credit.
- **Unresolved.** When the window passes without a decision, the EXIT is no longer shown as awaiting confirmation. It becomes an unresolved departure under `UNCONFIRMED_BOUNDARY` review and the visit ends at the EXIT. Its samples stay evidence only: they neither restore the visit through the 60-second bounce rule nor open a new one, so an early inside fix after a genuine departure cannot start credit. A later routine check or platform ENTER establishes current presence at its own time.

Each EXIT's verification is bounded by its own window: a later EXIT starts new verification and can never be rejected with samples taken before it.

A late delivery never erases attendance that was observed before the EXIT: the earlier visit keeps its ENTER-to-EXIT credit and only the unobserved interval after the EXIT is uncertain. Only a genuine capture outage, or another office's evidence since the visit opened, makes a later inside fix an unsafe recovery that splits the old visit without credit; later samples in the same verification then continue the new visit rather than splitting again.

After a rejection the platform may still consider the device outside and report no EXIT for a later real departure. The app therefore re-adds that office's geofence with the same request ID and an initial ENTER trigger, which Android documents as reporting ENTER only if the device is already inside; that ENTER carries its own observation time, corroborates the open visit and creates no earlier arrival. Whether Play services replaces the fence and restores its state this way has not been verified on a phone. Until the platform reports again, weekday checks record a corroborating inside observation while that visit stays open. If a later check finds the user outside, or the user is next observed at another office, credit runs only to the last same-office inside observation in the visit; the remaining span is uncredited under review. A visit with no inside evidence after its ENTER keeps the existing behaviour and earns nothing for the uncertain span.

### Process recreation versus capture outage

Android reclaims background app processes routinely without affecting geofences that Play services holds for the app. Registration is lost after a reboot, an app update, a data clear or a force-stop. Before Android 15 a force-stop does not cancel the app's PendingIntents, so a surviving token alone cannot rule one out; from Android 15 the system cancels them and also reports `wasForceStopped`. At the first capture work in a new process the app gathers, before re-registering:

- whether the geofence PendingIntent still exists, and whether the boot count and app update time still match those recorded at the last successful registration;
- on Android 11 and later, why the previous process ended, if that record was written after the last confirmed capture health;
- on Android 15 and later, whether this start followed a force-stop;
- whether the work began from a background job, which a force-stopped app cannot run until the user opens it.

Any sign of loss (missing token, reboot, update, force-stop, or a previous exit recorded as a user stop, permission change or package change) is an outage. Registration is treated as continuous only with positive evidence: a background start, a present token on Android 15 and later, or on Android 11–14 a recorded system reclaim of the last healthy process. Everything else, including a foreground launch on Android 8–10 or any unreadable signal, keeps the outage behaviour: dates since the last confirmed health become unknown and a new observation is required before presence is confirmed. A force-stop applied on Android 11–14 while no process was running leaves no exit record and is not detectable this way.

A synthetic stationary example illustrates the rules: ENTER 09:00, EXIT 11:00 delivered 30 seconds later, and inside samples at 11:01, 11:02, 11:03 and 11:04 reject the EXIT, so the visit continues from 09:00 with one arrival grace. If the samples are bunched within one minute, or the first one comes after 11:03, the EXIT is unresolved after 11:05:30, credit ends at 11:00 and the samples start nothing; the next routine check establishes current presence. If the visit continues after a rejection and a weekday check at 12:30 finds the user outside, with a corroborating inside check at 12:00, credit runs to 12:00 and 12:00–12:30 is reviewable. Tests cover these sequences plus delayed delivery, bunched and contradictory samples, cross-office movement and process recreation. Physical-phone stationary boundary, genuine departure, poor indoor accuracy, background/screen-off, reboot, force-stop, Wi-Fi variation and OEM battery behaviour remain unverified. No location or attendance is uploaded and no Physical Activity permission is added.
